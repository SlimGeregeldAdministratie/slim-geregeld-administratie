For the year-end close (jaarafsluiting) of your Dutch sole trader business (eenmanszaak), send your receipts, purchase and sales invoices, bank statements and an overview of unpaid invoices. If you hold stock, include a supported closing stock figure, and identify personal withdrawals, personal contributions and business purchases paid from your personal account. These records let me check both your profit and the movement in your business equity. Any additional documents depend on what your business does.

## Which receipts, invoices and bank records should you provide?

Provide the underlying documents together with the records showing how you entered and paid them. Keep purchase invoices (inkoopfacturen), sales invoices (verkoopfacturen) and receipts (bonnen) identifiable, so I can follow a transaction from the bookkeeping entry to its evidence. Include bank statements (bankafschriften) alongside those documents. For each bank payment, check which document or explanation supports it.

My working check is to match each bank payment to its supporting record, separate personal withdrawals from expenses, and record stock and business assets separately. That is the checking approach behind this handover. Use it while gathering your records to make clear how the files fit together. Where a payment is personal, label it clearly so it does not get treated as a business purchase.

Include an overview of outstanding items (openstaande posten), covering customer invoices still unpaid and supplier invoices you still need to pay. Identify which invoice each outstanding amount belongs to. Keep that overview alongside the invoices themselves. This gives me a clear question to resolve if the invoice record and payment record do not match.

## Can you hand over a spreadsheet and separate PDFs?

Yes, you can hand over your spreadsheet together with the supporting PDF invoices and receipts. A recurring question in my files is whether keeping the books in a spreadsheet is a problem. In those situations, I work from the spreadsheet and separate documents towards a year-end file whose entries can be checked. The useful starting point is a clear connection between each entry and its evidence.

Keep your existing descriptions and make the document references understandable. If a spreadsheet entry says only “payment”, add enough information to identify the relevant purchase or transfer. Where you already have a PDF, connect it to the entry rather than leaving me to guess which file belongs to it. This is also where you should flag transactions that you have not yet classified.

The handover should preserve what you know about your own records. Add an explanation beside an uncertain entry instead of choosing a category just to make the sheet look finished. Send the supporting document with that explanation. That gives me something concrete to review when preparing the year-end figures.

## How should you separate personal transfers and business expenses?

Separate money taken for personal use from business expenses, and identify money you have put into the business yourself. The Dutch Tax Administration explains that [personal withdrawals (privéonttrekkingen) reduce business equity and are not deductible expenses, while personal contributions (privéstortingen) increase equity without becoming business profit](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/winst/inkomstenbelasting/inkomstenbelasting_voor_ondernemers/privestortingen_en_priveonttrekkingen). For your handover, give those transfers recognisable descriptions. Keep them separate from customer receipts and supplier payments.

Several clients paid business expenses from personal accounts before their business bank accounts existed. Include the invoices and payment evidence for those purchases in your year-end documents. Mark that you paid personally, so I can check the business expense and the corresponding contribution together. Do not leave those records outside the handover simply because the payment is absent from the business bank statement.

## What should you provide if you hold stock or own equipment?

Provide a supported closing stock figure, and keep the details of business assets separate from stock. For closing stock (eindvoorraad), send the underlying list and explain how you arrived at the amount. For business assets (bedrijfsmiddelen), keep the purchase documents identifiable in a separate overview. The documents needed beyond that depend on your business.

Avoid combining everything you own into an unexplained total. Label the stock records and equipment records so I can see what each figure represents. If your spreadsheet already separates these categories, preserve that distinction in the handover. Add the supporting files alongside the relevant overview.

## How do profit and personal withdrawals connect to closing equity?

Check closing equity by starting with opening equity, adding profit and personal contributions, and subtracting personal withdrawals. The [balance sheet (balans) shows the business position at a particular moment, while the profit and loss account (winst-en-verliesrekening) shows annual revenue and costs](https://www.kvk.nl/producten-bestellen/welke-gegevens-staan-er-in-een-jaarrekening/). Business equity (eigen vermogen) needs its own reconciliation alongside the profit calculation. That is why the personal transfers belong in the year-end review.

This pattern comes up in several files. A worked example with round amounts:

| Movement | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Opening equity | EUR 5,000 |
| Add profit | EUR 8,000 |
| Subtract personal withdrawals | EUR 6,000 |
| Closing equity | EUR 7,000 |

The calculation follows the opening position through to the closing position. Personal withdrawals appear separately in that movement. Keep the transfer records available alongside the profit figures so I can check how the closing amount was reached.

My advice: deliver a complete, traceable set of records and leave uncertain entries visibly flagged. Include personally paid business purchases and supporting stock records where relevant. Start with your bank payments and attach the matching document or a clear explanation to each entry.
