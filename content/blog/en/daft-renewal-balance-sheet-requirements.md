For DAFT visa renewal, the IND requests a balance sheet (balans) and profit and loss account (winst-en-verliesrekening). These must cover the current and previous financial year and receive external expert checking. A bank statement alone is insufficient under the [treaty renewal checklist](https://ind.nl/en/forms/7535.pdf).

## What documents do forms 7035 and 7535 request?

Both forms request financial statements covering the same periods for treaty-based renewals. The table below compares their financial evidence requirements.

| Form | Language | Financial periods requested | Required checking |
|---|---|---|---|
| [7035](https://ind.nl/nl/formulieren/7035.pdf) | Dutch | Current and previous financial year | Authorised external expert |
| [7535](https://ind.nl/en/forms/7535.pdf) | English | Current and previous financial year | Authorised external expert |

These are renewal application forms with sections for different work-related permits. The relevant financial checklist sits under the American and Japanese treaty category. Both request evidence showing that the invested capital has been maintained.

## Which two financial years should your statements cover?

The forms specify the current financial year and the previous financial year. They expressly allow an interim balance sheet, so the request includes figures from an unfinished year. You can check that wording in the [Dutch renewal form](https://ind.nl/nl/formulieren/7035.pdf).

For your financial year (boekjaar), I check the accounting period used in your records. I also ask you to confirm your application timing and the periods already recorded in your bookkeeping.

I then establish which figures are complete and which need updating. Before I prepare the documents, I ask for the records covering both requested periods.

In several enquiries, people needed clarification about which years to supply. My practical starting point is to write the reporting periods down before collecting the supporting records.

## Why does a bank statement fall short?

A bank statement cannot provide the complete financial picture requested for renewal. The treaty checklist explicitly says it is insufficient.

A [balance sheet records the business’s financial position](https://www.kvk.nl/en/ordering-products/financial-statements-what-information-can-i-find-in-financial-statements/) at a particular moment. The profit and loss account summarises revenue and expenses over a period.

Across several renewal enquiries, people asked whether their bank statement would be enough. I explain that the renewal documents cover both business activity and the capital remaining in the business.

In my work, I check equity (eigen vermogen), meaning business assets minus business debts. Private withdrawals and a first-year loss can reduce that equity before renewal.

That is why I check equity quarterly in my Compliance Plan. If your records need attention, I want to identify the missing information while the supporting documents are still available.

## Who may prepare and sign the financial evidence?

The renewal checklist requires an authorised external expert and names an accountant as an example.

For additional context, the treaty section of [IND form 7524](https://ind.nl/en/forms/7524.pdf) explicitly includes bookkeepers and financial advisers among independent external experts. That form concerns applications for self-employment, so I keep its purpose separate from the renewal checklist.

## Does the first-application procedure change your renewal requirements?

The first-application procedure has a different timetable from renewal. The IND’s [treaty guidance](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/residence-permit-self-employed-person) explains the procedure introduced in April 2024. First-time treaty applicants without an existing Dutch residence permit receive six months for registration and investment, starting from the date on their decision letter.

For renewal, that guidance says the IND checks whether the business remained active and the invested capital stayed in the business. Your annual accounts (jaarrekening) provide financial evidence for that assessment.

I therefore keep the opening balance sheet as a starting record. It documents the initial investment and gives me a reference when preparing renewal figures.
