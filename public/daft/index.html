First, the thing almost everyone gets wrong

Get this one right and the rest of your paperwork becomes simple. Get it wrong and your application can be rejected while your bank account looks perfectly fine.

The common assumption

"I need to keep €4,500 sitting in my business bank account."

What the IND actually requires

€4,500 of owner's equity on your balance sheet. Equity is what you put into the business minus what the business owes. Your bank balance is only one part of that sum.

Why a signature is required

A bank statement does not prove equity, so the IND wants an authorised external professional to state on their professional responsibility that the €4,500 is really there and really yours.

You do not need a registeraccountant

The IND form names four kinds of authorised expert: a registeraccountant, an accountant-administratieconsulent, a bookkeeper or a financial adviser. An RA is built for auditing large companies and is expensive overkill. A bookkeeper is on the list, and that is what you get here.

Why so few firms do it separately

Most offices only take this on inside a yearly package, or turn it down as too small. Here it is a genuine standalone service with a price and a date attached.

Who can use this route

Two nationalities get the same favourable treatment, and the paperwork is identical for both.

🇺🇸

US citizens

The Dutch American Friendship Treaty, known as DAFT. It is purpose 453 on the IND application form: no points system, €4,500 of equity, and a balance sheet checked and signed by an authorised professional.

🇯🇵

Japanese nationals

The 1913 Dutch-Japanese Trade Treaty gives the same rights through its most favoured nation clause. It is purpose 510 on the same form, with identical requirements, and it is handled here just as often.

🌍

Other nationalities

The IND names only these two nationalities for this route. Other non-EU nationals fall under the regular self-employed permit with the RVO points system, and Turkish nationals have their own route that asks for a business plan instead. Ask and you get a straight answer about which applies to you.

What the IND wants to see, per legal form

Straight from the application form. Which list applies depends on how your business is registered, and the balance sheet is on all three.

Eenmanszaak

Your KVK registration, a business bank statement showing the invested amount, and the opening balance sheet. This is the situation most treaty applicants are in.

Vennootschap onder firma

A deed or contract stating each partner's financial share, the opening balance sheet, and a business bank statement. Note that the equity requirement counts per partner.

BV or NV

The deed of incorporation, the opening balance sheet, and a business bank statement. More paperwork, and the balance sheet still needs an authorised signature.

On the bank statement the IND wants both your business name and the invested capital visible. A statement that shows only an account number and an amount gets sent back, which is one of the easiest delays to avoid.

Four mistakes that quietly break the €4,500

Each of these looks harmless and each one can drop your equity below the threshold. They are the reason it pays to have someone look at the numbers before you submit.

1

Borrowing the money

A loan is debt, not equity, so it does not count no matter whose account it sits in. A gift is different: money given with no obligation to repay becomes yours, and yours can go in as equity.

2

Buying gear right after depositing

Equipment is worth less than you paid the moment you own it. Buy a laptop the week after your deposit and your equity can already be under the line.

3

Spending the VAT you collected

Part of what your clients pay you is VAT you owe the tax office. Withdraw the full invoice amount and you have spent money that was never yours.

4

Only involving a bookkeeper twice

Once at the start and once at renewal means nobody is watching the equity in between. That gap is where most renewal problems are created.

Questions people actually ask

Is the €4,500 about my bank balance or something else?

This is the single most common misunderstanding. The IND asks for €4,500 of owner's equity on your balance sheet, which is not the same as €4,500 sitting in your bank account. Equity is what you put into the business minus what the business owes. That is exactly why the document has to be drawn up by a bookkeeper: someone has to state professionally that the equity is really there.

Who can use this route: only Americans?

US citizens use the Dutch American Friendship Treaty (DAFT). Japanese nationals get the same favourable treatment through the 1913 Dutch-Japanese Trade Treaty and its most favoured nation clause: same exemption from the points system, same €4,500 equity requirement, same signed balance sheet. Both are handled here. Other non-EU nationals normally fall under the regular self-employed permit with the RVO points system, which is different paperwork. Turkish nationals have their own route under the EU-Turkey Association Agreement, which asks for a business plan rather than this balance sheet.

What if the money is a gift from family or friends?

A genuine gift counts as equity. The moment money is given to you with no obligation to repay it, it becomes your own money, and your own money can go into the business as equity. Two practical points. Get a short written confirmation from the giver stating that it is a gift and not a loan, so it is documented for both the IND and the tax office. And check Dutch gift tax: exemptions are generous between parents and children but much smaller for friends and third parties, so a gift from a friend can carry a tax bill. Mention it in the form and you get this sorted before the money moves.

What if the €4,500 is a loan?

Borrowed money is debt, not equity, so a balance sheet based on a loan does not meet the IND requirement. This comes up often and it is usually fixable, for example by using savings instead or by having the lender give the money rather than lend it. Flag it in the form and you get an explanation of your options before you move any money.

Who is allowed to sign it?

The IND application form asks for "an authorised independent external expert" and then names them: a registeraccountant, an accountant-administratieconsulent, a bookkeeper, or a financial adviser. So a bookkeeper is explicitly on the list, and you do not need an expensive registeraccountant, which exists for auditing large companies. Two words in that sentence matter: independent and external. Your accountant cannot be you, your partner or a family member. Slim Geregeld is an external practice registered with the Dutch Tax Administration, so both boxes are ticked.

What exactly does the IND want on the bank statement?

Both your business name and the invested capital have to be visible on it. A statement showing only an account number and a balance is a common reason for a request to be sent back for more information, which costs you weeks. If your bank's export does not show the business name, say so and we will sort out a version that does.

Do I need to deposit the €4,500 before you can start?

The money needs to be in the business before the final version can be signed, because the balance sheet has to reflect your actual position. You can send your request before that. Your file is prepared in the meantime and the signed PDF follows as soon as your deposit is visible.

Can equipment count towards the €4,500?

Yes, business assets such as a laptop or camera gear can be part of your equity, but be careful. Equipment is valued at its business worth and starts depreciating immediately, so buying gear right after depositing can push your equity below the threshold. Tell us what you are bringing in and it is valued and listed correctly.
